# Synthetic CivicAssist pilot assessment

## Decision

The supplied synthetic evidence supports a limited, human-supervised pilot rather than a full replacement of existing appointment channels. This is a recommendation, not a sourced fact.

## Evidence window

The reporting period runs from January through June 2030 and the evidence cutoff is June 30, 2030. The July bulletin (`SYN-S3`) falls outside the cutoff and is not used to support the decision.

## Findings

In the synthetic survey, 310 of 500 respondents said they would try the assistant, or 62% (`SYN-S1`). The same survey found that 405 respondents, or 81%, required a human option, while 180, or 36%, reported a need for language support.

The synthetic operations log records 3,070 completed appointments from 5,090 scheduled appointments during the period, or approximately 60% (`SYN-S2`). This administrative completion rate is not comparable to stated willingness: the measures use different constructs and populations.

The accessibility interview note (`SYN-S4`) suggests that channel choice, plain language, and assisted booking matter, but its identifier is incomplete. Treat it as qualified contextual evidence rather than a basis for a numerical claim.

## Analysis

Interest is sufficient to test a bounded service, but the stronger signal is the demand for human choice. A pilot that hides or removes staff-assisted access would contradict the supplied evidence. The operations baseline provides a measurement reference, not proof that an assistant will improve completion.

## Recommendation

Run a six-week opt-in pilot with a visible human handoff, language-support paths, and no reduction in existing channels. Measure successful booking completion, abandonment, error recovery, assisted handoffs, accessibility issues, and satisfaction by segment. Define a stop rule for harmful disparities or unresolved appointment errors.

## Limitations

All evidence is synthetic. The survey measures stated willingness rather than observed adoption. The operations log contains no causal comparison. One contextual source has an incomplete identifier. No post-cutoff evidence supports this assessment.
